Proposed study, not achieved performance

The U/R/A/C weighting comparison, calibrated reliability signals, source-level holdouts and human-review intervention are proposed work. C versus R is the primary comparison; the candidate labels, splits and training budgets must be matched. Calibration requires independently reviewed development labels. Scores do not substitute for human verification.

The 20-page pilot and approximately 200–300-page reviewed study are planning scopes, not collected data or proof of statistical power. Four arms with three matched training seeds require 12 initial runs; seeds are not independent source populations. Expansion toward 5,000 reviewed pages is contingent on permissions, observed throughput and agreed staffing. The 3,450-team-hour calculation is a scenario, not a measured workload.

A useful-gain threshold and quality margin should be agreed before testing. Report an unfavorable or inconclusive primary result honestly. Controlled diagnostics may explain it, but must not silently replace the primary comparison.

Role expectations and private confirmations

The annual research and teaching figures in the talk reflect a locally supplied role brief. They are planning requirements, not completed achievements or guaranteed acceptances. Confirm the employer’s current rules, resources and responsibilities directly. Local candidate confirmation; evidence withheld from repository.

The candidate must privately confirm individual research responsibilities, professional eligibility, actual availability and any future supervision agreement. Personal records and administrative details do not belong to this scientific claim ledger. Nothing here certifies a qualification, appointment, publication acceptance or completed human rehearsal.

Publication boundary

The script deliberately omits unsupported project benchmarks, award claims and unconfirmed implementation ownership. Withholding personal evidence does not convert a private assertion into a publicly verified claim. The repository edition preserves scientific definitions, references and proposed methods while keeping those evidence classes separate.
